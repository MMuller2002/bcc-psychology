Key Vocabulary
Bio-Psycho-Social Model
A framework explaining behaviour through biological, psychological, and social factors.
Fight-Flight-Freeze
The body's automatic response to perceived threat — preparing to fight, run, or become still.
HPA Axis
Hypothalamus–Pituitary–Adrenal axis: the hormone pathway activated during stress.
Cortisol
A stress hormone released by the adrenal cortex that regulates energy and immune response.
Adaptive Coping
Strategies that help manage stress effectively without causing harm.
Maladaptive Coping
Strategies that provide short-term relief but worsen wellbeing over time.

The Stress Response
Your Body on High Alert

By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to explain the fight-flight-freeze response, name the key stress hormones, and trace the HPA axis pathway from stressor to body reaction.

⏱ 70 minutes 🎯 Explain 🖊 Body Mapping
⚡
Hook — Freeze Frame Experience
0–10 min · Whole class
▼

Your teacher gave you 45 seconds to list capital cities. Now reflect on what your body actually did during that moment:

🫀 Body Check-In

You just experienced a mild stress response. Today's lesson explains exactly what your body just did — and why.

🔬
Direct Instruction — HPA Axis & Hormones
10–28 min · Notes
▼

The stress response involves two pathways — one fast (seconds) and one slower (minutes). Both start in the brain.

🧪 The Two Pathways
⚡ FAST PATH (seconds): Sympathetic Nervous System

Stressor → Amygdala → Hypothalamus → Adrenal medulla → Adrenaline + Noradrenaline

Effects: ↑ heart rate, ↑ breathing, ↑ blood pressure, dilated pupils, muscle blood flow

🐢 SLOW PATH (minutes): HPA Axis

Hypothalamus → Pituitary gland → Adrenal cortex → Cortisol

Effects: releases glucose, suppresses immune system (short term), impairs memory under chronic stress

🧊 Don't Forget: The FREEZE Response

The freeze response is high adrenaline combined with motor system shutdown — like a 'play dead' reflex. This is why people freeze during exams, panic attacks, or traumatic experiences. Your brain cannot tell the difference between a lion and an exam.

🎨

Stress & Coping
What Do We Actually Do?

By the end of this lesson, you'll be able to analyse adaptive and maladaptive coping strategies and examine how societal values shape which strategies get researched and promoted.

⏱ 70 minutes 🎯 Analyse ✍️ TEEL Paragraph
🎭
Hook — What Do You Actually Do?
0–10 min · Anonymous
▼

Your teacher will collect anonymous responses and read them aloud. After hearing the class results, sort the strategies into two columns:

✅ Approach Strategies
❌ Avoidance Strategies

Key question to think about: Who decides which of these is healthy — and is that always accurate?

📖
Direct Instruction — Types of Coping
10–25 min · Notes
▼

There are three main categories of coping. The same strategy can be adaptive or maladaptive depending on context.

1️⃣ PROBLEM-FOCUSED COPING

Directly addresses the stressor itself. Examples: making a study plan, seeking information, assertiveness, time management.

2️⃣ EMOTION-FOCUSED COPING

Adaptive: exercise, mindfulness, journaling, social support, creative expression.
Maladaptive: substance use, avoidance, rumination, aggression.

3️⃣ MEANING-FOCUSED COPING

Finding purpose or reframing the stressor. Examples: faith/prayer, gratitude, post-traumatic growth, community connection.

🔗 AC9S10H04 Connection

Why do mindfulness and CBT get more research funding than prayer or community rituals? What does this reveal about whose values shape scientific research priorities?

Critical Thinking — Wall of Wisdom
45–58 min · Sticky notes
▼

Your teacher will share three research facts. For each one, write your response on your sticky note or below:

Fact 1

Most early stress research was conducted on male rats and male human participants.

Fact 2

The 'tend and befriend' stress response was only formally described in 2000 — adding female-typical responses to the fight-flight-freeze model.

Fact 3

Indigenous and culturally-specific coping strategies (e.g. connection to Country) are rarely included in mainstream stress research.
