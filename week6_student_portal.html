Key Terms
FINER Criteria
Feasible, Interesting, Novel, Ethical, Relevant — the framework for evaluating research questions.
Operationalisation
Making abstract variables concrete and measurable — specifying exactly how the IV and DV will be recorded.
Directional Hypothesis
Predicts both that a difference exists AND its direction (higher/lower). Requires an evidential warrant.
Null Hypothesis (H₀)
States no relationship exists — the default assumption that statistics work to disprove.
Evidential Warrant
The research evidence that justifies a directional prediction — what makes a hypothesis more than a guess.
Confounding Variable
A variable that independently affects the DV, creating an alternative explanation for findings.
Correlational Design
Measures the relationship between naturally-occurring variables without manipulation. Cannot establish causation.

Research Questions —
The Art of Asking Well

By the end of this lesson you'll have a Version 2 research question that passes the FINER criteria — refined through the Ladder of Feedback peer review process.

// 70 minFINER CriteriaPZ: Ladder of Feedback
📋 Week 6 Assessment target: By the end of Lesson 3 today you need a research question, a hypothesis, and a draft method. Everything this week builds directly toward your Week 8 spoken investigation.
🔍
Hook — What Makes a Question Researchable?
0–10 min · Class discussion
▼

Your teacher will open by reading some of the "Challenge" questions you submitted at the end of Week 5. Before instruction, evaluate these five questions — are they good research questions or flawed ones?

"Why does sleep affect teenagers?"
"Does more sleep lead to better grades?"
"I want to investigate stress in students."
"Among Year 10 students, does nightly sleep duration relate to self-rated academic alertness the following morning?"
"Does social media affect mental health?"
A good research question has this structure: 'Among [population], does [IV — measured by ____] relate to / predict [DV — measured by ____]?' — it must contain an IV, a DV, a population, and genuine uncertainty.
📐
Direct Instruction — The FINER Criteria
10–27 min · Notes · Resource Sheet 16
▼

A good research question passes all five FINER tests. Evaluate your draft question against each criterion by clicking the buttons below:

F
Feasible
Can it be investigated with the resources, time, and participants available to a Year 10 student? (No fMRI, no 500 participants, no 6-month studies)
I
Interesting
Is there genuine uncertainty? Does it go beyond what is already well-established? "Does sleep affect performance?" is NOT interesting — the answer is already known.
N
Novel
Does it add something — a specific population (Year 10 at BCC), a specific context, or a specific operationalisation that makes it distinct?
E
Ethical
Can it be conducted without harm? Is consent manageable? Does it avoid sensitive data (medical history, identifiable personal information) without proper safeguards?
R
Relevant
Does it connect to existing psychology research? Would the findings have implications worth caring about for your cohort or for the field?
✍️
Draft + Ladder of Feedback (PZ Routine)
27–58 min · Individual + Pairs · Resource Sheet 16
▼

Choose a topic, draft your Version 1 question, then swap with a partner for Ladder of Feedback peer review. Complete all four rungs before writing Version 2.

1
Clarify — "I'm not sure what you mean by..."
Ask before judging. What in your partner's question needs clarification? No assumptions.
2
Value — "What works here is..."
Genuine strengths only — before any criticism.
3
Concerns — "I wonder whether..."
Questions, not statements. What worries you about the question?
4
Suggest — "What if you tried..."
One or two concrete, actionable improvements only.
✏️

58–70 min · Written submission
▼

Submit your final research question with a 3-sentence rationale. This is handed to your teacher and will be returned with FINER feedback before Lesson 2.

Lesson 2 · AC9S10I07 · Construct

Hypothesis Development —
Committing to a Prediction

By the end of this lesson you'll have a directional hypothesis with all five components — including an evidential warrant. A hypothesis with no evidence behind it is just a guess.

// 70 min5-Component AnatomyPZ: What Makes You Say That?
📬
Hook — FINER Feedback Review
0–10 min · Individual review
▼

Your teacher returns your research questions with FINER feedback. Read it carefully, make any final revisions, and confirm your question is locked in. Then:

Your research question asks what you want to find out. Your hypothesis is your answer before you collect data — your prediction, based on evidence. Today you make that commitment.
🏗
Direct Instruction — Five-Component Anatomy
10–27 min · Notes · Resource Sheet 17
▼
Template: '[Population] who [IV — condition A, measured by ____] will show significantly [higher/lower] [DV, measured by ____] than those who [IV — condition B], because [research finding + mechanism].'
Component 1 — Specific population: Year 10 students at BCC
Component 2 — IV with both conditions: who report ≥8 hrs sleep (7-day diary) vs <8 hrs
Component 3 — DV with measurement: will score higher on the academic alertness scale (1–10 Likert)
Component 4 — Predicted direction: higher / lower / more / fewer / greater than
Component 5 — Evidential warrant: as demonstrated by [Author, Year] who found [mechanism]... This is what separates a hypothesis from a guess.
Which of these is a properly operationalised DV?
🔬
Guided Practice — Sample Hypothesis Evaluation
27–42 min · Individual · Resource Sheet 17
▼

Evaluate these six sample hypotheses on Resource Sheet 17. Below, rewrite the weakest one:

WEAK 1: "Students who use their phone before bed will sleep worse." (Missing: population, measurement, evidential warrant)
STRONG 2: "Year 10 students who report >2 hrs social media after 9pm (diary) will score significantly lower on PSQI than those reporting <30 mins, as Hale & Guan (2015) found a dose-response relationship between evening screen use and sleep disturbance."
WEAK 3: "There will be a difference in stress between students who exercise and those who don't." (Non-directional — but the literature clearly supports a direction. Why is it non-directional?)
STRONG 4: "Year 10 students doing ≥3 weekly exercise sessions will score significantly lower on PSS-10 than those with <1 session, consistent with meta-analytic evidence (Stubbs et al., 2017) showing exercise reduces perceived stress in adolescents."
💬
PZ Routine — What Makes You Say That?
42–55 min · Individual writing
▼

Before writing your hypothesis, complete this two-part routine. If you cannot complete Part 2, your prediction is a guess — not a hypothesis. Find the research first, then write the direction.

WHAT MAKES YOU SAY THAT? — Project Zero Thinking Routine
I predict that...
State your predicted direction — what do you expect to find? Be specific about IV, DV, and direction.
What makes me say that is...
What research evidence or theory justifies this prediction? Name at least ONE study. This is your evidential warrant.
✏️
Exit — Write and Peer-Check Your Hypothesis
55–70 min · Written + peer review
▼

Write your directional hypothesis, then tick each component as your partner checks it.

1

2

3
DV stated with measurement approach
4
Predicted direction specified (higher / lower / more / fewer)
5
Evidential warrant — at least one research finding cited

Research Design —
From Question to Method

By the end of this lesson you'll have a draft method plan — specifying design type, participants, IV/DV operationalisation, confounds and controls, and a replicable procedure.

// 70 minIV · DV · Controls · ProcedureDevil's Advocate peer review
⚠️
Hook — What Could Go Wrong?
0–10 min · Design flaw identification
▼

Identify the design flaw in each of these real research errors. Click the flaw type you think it is:

Flaw 1
Researcher surveyed students about stress immediately after exam results were released.
Flaw 2
The high-sleep group averaged 9.2 hours per night — implausibly high for Year 10 students.
Flaw 3
Exercise group recruited from school sports teams; control group from a maths enrichment program.
Flaw 4
Academic performance measured using assignment grades from different teachers with different grading scales.
🗺
Direct Instruction — Components of a Valid Design
10–27 min · Notes · Resource Sheet 18
▼
Design type: Most Year 10 investigations will be correlational (measuring naturally-occurring variables, no manipulation) or quasi-experimental (comparing pre-existing groups). Neither can establish causation — but both can establish meaningful relationships.
Operationalisation: "Academic performance" is not measurable. "Self-reported academic alertness on a 1–10 scale measured the morning after each diary entry" is. Every variable needs an instrument, a scale, and a timing.
Confounds: What else could explain a change in your DV, independent of your IV? You need at least 3. Identifying them is not a weakness — it is methodological sophistication.
The alignment check: The single most important design question — does my method actually answer my research question? If the IV in the method doesn't match the IV in the hypothesis, the study cannot answer what it claims to investigate.

Devil's Advocate Peer Review
47–58 min · Pairs
▼

Present your design to your partner in 2 minutes. Your partner's job is to attack it — find every weakness in 3 minutes. Record what they raised:

"How do you know participants are answering honestly?"
"What if a confound I haven't thought of affects your results?"
"Could someone else run this study exactly from your procedure?"
