Key Terms
Hypnogram
A graph showing sleep stages across a night — the visual "fingerprint" of your sleep architecture.
Ultradian Cycle
The ~90-minute repeating cycle through N1→N2→N3→REM sleep, occurring 4–6 times per night.
Sleep Spindles
Brief 12–15 Hz bursts of neural activity in N2 — linked to memory consolidation and noise suppression.
REM Atonia
Active muscle paralysis during REM sleep — prevents you from physically acting out your dreams.
Glymphatic System
The brain's waste-clearance network — most active during N3 deep sleep; flushes amyloid-beta.
Polysomnography (PSG)
Multi-channel sleep study recording EEG, eye movements, muscle tone, heart rate, and breathing simultaneously.
Actigraphy
Wrist-worn movement sensor that infers sleep/wake patterns — the technology behind Fitbit sleep tracking.

⚠️ Common Mistake

REM is often called "deep sleep" by the public — this is wrong. REM is the most physiologically active stage. The brain is essentially awake; only the body is paralysed. N3 is the correct "deep sleep."

🔄 The Ultradian Cycle

A ~90-minute cycle through all stages repeats 4–6 times per night. Early cycles: heavy N3. Late cycles: heavy REM. An alarm at 6 hours removes the final cycles — the ones richest in emotional and cognitive processing.

⚠️ The Dose-Response Relationship

17–19 hours awake = cognitive impairment equivalent to a blood alcohol content of 0.05% — the legal driving limit. Two weeks of 6-hour nights matches one full night of total sleep deprivation in performance — but people rate themselves as "only slightly tired." The subjective sense of impairment is lost before the actual impairment is.

🧠
Direct Instruction — Four Performance Domains
10–27 min · Notes
▼
Domain 1 — Attention & Processing Speed

PVT (Psychomotor Vigilance Task): 17–19 hrs awake = BAC 0.05% impairment. Reaction time degrades ~13ms per hour of deprivation beyond 16 hours. Critical finding: chronic restriction (6 hrs/night for 2 weeks) = same impairment as one sleepless night — but people feel only slightly tired. Awareness of impairment is lost before the impairment is.

Domain 2 — Memory Consolidation

Declarative memory requires N2 sleep spindles to transfer hippocampal encodings to cortical storage. Procedural memory requires N2/N3. REM "edits" emotional memory — strengthening the lesson while weakening the emotional sting. Cramming by cutting sleep is neurologically self-defeating.

Domain 3 — Emotional Regulation

Amygdala reactivity: 60% greater after one night of sleep deprivation (Walker et al., 2007). The PFC-amygdala circuit is the most sleep-sensitive in the brain. Bidirectional relationship: poor sleep → depression, and depression → poor sleep. 90% of depressed patients report sleep disturbance.

Domain 4 — Academic Performance

Wahlstrom et al. (2014): shifting start time from 7:15am → 8:40am produced a 1.06 GPA increase across 9,000 students. Carskadon: each earlier start hour reduces sleep by ~30 minutes (not compensated by earlier bedtime). ANOMALY: some studies find no GPA improvement — possible confounds: SES, family stability, individual chronotype variation.

Critical Thinking — The Correlation Trap
47–58 min · Discussion
▼

The sleep-performance correlation is solid. But correlation ≠ causation. Three alternative explanations:

Reverse Causation

Struggling students may sleep less because of stress about poor performance — making sleep deprivation the consequence, not the cause.

Common Cause (Third Variable)

Socioeconomic disadvantage independently reduces both sleep quality (noise, overcrowding, insecurity) and academic performance (under-resourced schools, low parental support).

Selection Bias

Students who describe themselves as "good sleepers" may also be more conscientious and organised — traits that independently predict academic performance regardless of sleep.

📜 Historical moment

In 1953, Nathaniel Kleitman and Eugene Aserinsky used EEG to discover REM sleep — they initially thought the equipment was broken when they saw "waking-like" brain activity in sleeping subjects. This single discovery overturned centuries of assumption that sleep was a passive, uniform state.

🔬
Direct Instruction — Four Measurement Methods
10–27 min · Notes · Resource Sheet 9
▼
MethodWhat it measuresStrengthKey limitation
EEG Cortical electrical activity via scalp electrodes — Hz and amplitude Objective · High temporal resolution · Identifies stages precisely Lab only · First-night effect · Equipment discomfort
PSG EEG + EOG (eye movements) + EMG (muscle) + ECG + respiratory + oximetry Gold standard · Diagnoses disorders (apnea, narcolepsy, RBD) $800–2000/night · Lab only · Unnatural conditions
Actigraphy Movement patterns via wrist accelerometer → infers sleep/wake Naturalistic · 7-day monitoring · Lower cost 80–90% accurate for sleep/wake only — cannot identify stages
Self-Report Subjective sleep quality (PSQI questionnaire, sleep diary) Free · Scalable · Captures subjective experience Retrospective bias · Poor insight (±20 min error on sleep onset)
⚠️ The Waking vs REM Problem

On EEG alone, waking and REM look nearly identical — both show desynchronised, low-amplitude beta waves. The ONLY way to distinguish them in a PSG is the EMG channel: during REM, the chin muscle goes flat (atonia). This is why PSG uses multiple channels — no single signal tells the full story.
